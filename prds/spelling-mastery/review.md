Reviewed: 2026-10-11 (verification pass on the revised PRD) · Stakes: hobby (full-length by stakeholder decision; a large dev team builds from it) · PRD status: in-review

## Verdict
The revision is substantial and faithful to the log. All nine review decisions (R1–R9) appear in the PRD. Every FR now has acceptance criteria, and the §4.7 transition table, FR-45, FR-74 and the worked day/date examples agree arithmetically. 25 of the 30 earlier findings are fully resolved, 5 are partially resolved, and none are still open. The PRD is **ready for design**. **Engineering can start on everything outside the learning engine and metrics.** Five new high findings need a short fix pass before engine build:
- Diagnostic-originated words aren't eligible under FR-76.
- Held-out words contradict FR-75.
- The FR-57 exposure rule starves SM-1.
- SM-6 is partly unreachable by month 3.
- The FR-78 spelling-correction workflow keeps drilling the misspelled word.

None of these needs a new interview round, but two of them (#33, #34) need a quick stakeholder answer.

## Dimension notes
### Problem clarity: strong
This is unchanged and still strong. §2 states the cost to the child and the parent, §2.3 is honest that the evidence is thin, and the thesis makes a falsifiable bet with numbers. The thesis now cites SM-6, which brings in the timing problem in #34.

### Decision-readiness: strong
R1 to R9 are all reflected precisely: the spacing and carry-over rule (R1), the diagnostic-correct path (R2), eligibility and order (R3), deadline precedence (R4), preload (R5), the AI switch (R6), versioning (R7), playtests (R8), and SM-6/SM-7 (R9). Nothing in the PRD contradicts the log. The one unlogged change is the SM-1 baseline (#52). Open questions have owners and blocking status that match §8. FR-73 no longer blocks the release.

### Substance over theater: strong
There is still no persona or NFR theater. The new FRs (FR-74 to FR-81) each serve the engine or the metrics, and FR-81 supports NFR-12 replay. The child experience principles are concrete enough to guide design.

### Strategic coherence: adequate
The features follow the thesis tightly, and the revision fixed the earlier structural problems: the unified pattern profile, the gated playtests and the cohort metric. The remaining weakness is in measurement. SM-1 now has too few eligible attempts because of the exposure definition (#33), and SM-6 can't be fully reached by its read date (#34). Both sit on the thesis, and both are cheap to fix.

### Done-ness: adequate
This is up from "thin". Every FR has acceptance criteria. I checked every worked date and day example arithmetically:
- FR-44: Mon → Tue → Wed, then Wed+3 = Sat, rolled to Mon.
- FR-74: Mon+3 = Thu, then Thu+7.
- FR-45: days 1/4/11/18 Mastered; days 1/3/14 not (13-day span); days 1/10/15 not (5-day gap). In the error case, the earliest mastering day is 27, because day 26 fails the 7-day gap.
- FR-41: 4 errors across 3 words = Moderate.
- FR-57: 26/30 = 87%.
- FR-58: +8 pp = Improving.
- FR-77: 4/5 = 80% = Secure.

All of these are consistent with their rules, apart from the day-0 wording in #46. The remaining gaps are interaction effects between rules: eligibility (#31), held-out words (#32), the correction workflow (#35), quota scope (#36), evidence types (#38), the remediation trigger (#39), and acceptance criteria that depend on unpublishable states (#43).

### Scope honesty: adequate
All 66 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed in §14, and every §14 entry has an inline tag. However, several of the new engine rules were invented by the author and are untagged (#44, and #33's exposure scope). The open-item density fits an in-review PRD with no date.

### Shape fit: strong
The PRD is about 890 lines, longer than the ~10–15 pages agreed in Round 6. The growth is almost entirely acceptance criteria and engine rules, which a large team needs, not padding.
